# Pre/post comparison of Q1 descriptors

Separate from the earlier post-interview analyses (nothing in `all_questions/`, `q3/` or `topic_network_post_allquestions/` is touched).

## Data
`data/consensus_pre_q1.csv` and `consensus_post_q1.csv`: rater-consensus descriptors of Q1 ("how do you feel now?"), split on `;` into one unit each (order kept). 150 pre + 125 post units after cleaning; 51 pre / 50 post participants, 49 with both (paired tests use those). Participant 33 has no condition and is excluded; participant 48 has no pre descriptors. Exact repeats within a participant/timepoint are dropped (1 pre, 1 post); rows flagged `needs_review` are kept (sensitivity runs drop them; that drops whole participants, leaving 31 paired). All logged in `outputs/prepost_audit.json`.

## Topic extraction: separate models per timepoint, shared model only where categories must match
`python3 fit_timepoint_topics.py --time pre` and `--time post` fit one BERTopic model per timepoint on that timepoint's descriptors only (same settings, min cluster size 5; 10 topics each, 10% / 13% unassigned), so a timepoint's topics are not shaped by the other interview. Topic labels for these models are in `prepost_common.py` (`TOPIC_LABELS_PRE`, `TOPIC_LABELS_POST`, interpretations written after reading sample descriptors). They are used for the core networks (`plot_core_network.py`: pre panels with pre topics, post panels with post topics) and by `../topic_network_pre/`. Tests that compare topic categories across timepoints (topic composition change, per-topic and PageRank change, flow-graph JSD pre vs post) need one common set of topics and therefore still use the shared-topic model of `prepare_and_model.py` (one BERTopic model on pre + post together); their figure rows are marked "(shared topics)". Embedding-space and relief-projection analyses do not use topics.

## Pipeline (run from this folder, in order)
1. `python3 prepare_and_model.py`: Qwen3 embeddings and one shared BERTopic model on pre + post pooled (11 topics + outliers); short topic labels are in `prepost_common.py` (an interpretation written after reading sample units; topics 2, 8, 9 overlap).
2. `python3 prepost_content_tests.py`: baseline, paired pre->post and condition x time tests for topic composition and semantic space (participant centroids, per-PC).
3. `python3 prepost_pagerank_tests.py`: PageRank per condition x time on the flow graphs, change tests, path-distribution JSD, interaction.
4. `python3 plot_prepost.py`: figures (in `prepost_semantic_space` the axes are the two PCs that separate pre from post most, PC3 and PC1, oriented so that post lies to the upper right, picked for display only; it keeps the two insets (heat table of the FDR-adjusted p-values; PCs that move with group means and end labels, no stars) and marks significance on the map itself: a * beside a condition's filled diamond marks a significant pre->post shift in semantic space (BH-FDR over the three conditions) and the PCs listed next to it are the ones that change significantly within that condition (BH over the first 10 PCs; sign-flipped PC3 shown) and 'Anticipation / Calm & video' (PC3) are my interpretation of the topic positions on each PC, not the full end-evidence procedure used for the post-interview PCs). Sensitivity: add `--drop-review --tag _sens_no_review` to steps 2 and 3.

## Tests
All permutation tests with participants as the unit. Baseline: condition labels permuted (pre only). Time: sign flips (pre/post swap) within participant. Interaction: condition labels permuted across participants' paired change vectors (PERMANOVA on the change, ANOVA per topic/PC). FDR (Benjamini-Hochberg) throughout: over topics, over the first 10 PCs and over pairs.

## Between-condition test of the pooled results
`python3 prepost_between_condition.py` (after step 2): for the measures behind the pooled pre->post effect (topic composition, semantic space, share of Anxiety & fear / Video & nature / Feeling calmer, PC1, PC3), is the change different between conditions? Permutation of condition labels across participants (10000); contrasts VR Art vs VR Only, VR Art vs Control, VR Only vs Control (BH-FDR over the three) and the planned combined VR (Art + Only) vs Control (BH over measures). Univariate effects have participant-bootstrap CIs; multivariate effects are reported without a CI (a bootstrapped norm is biased upward). Output: `prepost_between_condition*.csv`, `prepost_between_condition.*`.

## Results (main run)
* Baseline: no condition differences (composition p = .52, semantic space p = .65, PageRank p = .37).
* Pre -> post, all 49 paired participants: strong shift in composition (p = .001) and semantic space (p < .001). Anxiety & fear falls (26% -> 9% of descriptors, BH p = .02); Video & nature and Feeling calmer rise. PC1 (worry -> calm/fine) and PC3 (optimism/expecting it to go well/anxiety -> calm/video/relaxed) move.
* By condition: VR Art and VR Only shift (semantic p = .002 / .003), Control does not reach significance (p = .18).
* Condition x time interaction: not significant (composition p = .76, semantic p = .23, PageRank p = .98; VR Only vs Control semantic p = .07). "Significant in VR, not in Control" is not itself evidence that VR changes more.
* Between conditions, on the pooled headline results: nothing is reliably different. Anxiety & fear falls more in VR Only (-26 points) and VR Art (-16) than in Control (-9), but the differences are not significant (VR vs Control p = .34). Video & nature rises more in VR Art (+18 points) than in Control (0; p = .06 raw, .19 BH-FDR). PC3 moves further in VR Art (p = .10 raw, .29 BH-FDR). VR vs Control combined: all p >= .09 raw, >= .25 adjusted.
* PageRank pre vs post: no topic change survives correction. There are only 16-23 transitions per condition and timepoint, so the PageRank results are descriptive.
* Sensitivity (drop `needs_review`): pooled time effects hold, VR Art loses power (n = 9), interaction stays null.

Outputs in `outputs/`: figures `prepost_topic_shares`, `prepost_semantic_space`, `prepost_pagerank`, `prepost_tests_overview`; tables `prepost_content_tests*.csv`, `prepost_topic_changes*.csv`, `prepost_pc_changes*.csv`, `prepost_pagerank*.csv`, `prepost_units_with_topics.csv`.

## Core networks (`plot_core_network.py`)
Same design as `topic_network_post/outputs/core_network_within_response.*` (most central topic in the middle by PageRank, next three on an inner ring, the rest on an outer ring; node size and colour = PageRank; arrows = topic transitions between consecutive descriptors of one participant's Q1 list, i.e. "within response"). Outputs: `core_network_within_response_pre.*` (pre interviews, one panel per condition) and `core_network_pre_vs_post.*` (pre in the top row, post in the bottom row, shared PageRank scale). Each timepoint uses its own topics (the pre and post panels show different topic sets), so the topics are not those of the post-interview analysis in `topic_network_post/`. Unlike the topic_network figure, arrows are drawn for every observed transition (counts of 1 included), because each panel has only 16-23 transitions in total.

## Jensen-Shannon tests on the flow graphs (`prepost_path_jsd_tests.py`, ~1 min; figure `prepost_path_jsd.*`)
Each (condition, timepoint) flow graph is a Markov chain over the 11 topics (additive smoothing 0.1); with one shared opening-topic distribution (pooled over all participants and timepoints) it assigns a probability to every 3-step path, and the tests use the JSD (bits) between such path distributions. Within condition: JSD between a condition's pre and post graph, paired participants, null by swapping pre/post within participant. Between conditions: JSD between two conditions' graphs at pre and at post (plus the generalised JSD of all three), null by permuting condition labels across the participants of that timepoint. Interaction (does the change differ by condition?): difference in the amount of change |JSD(A) - JSD(B)| and the total-variation distance between the two conditions' change vectors (post minus pre path distributions), labels permuted across paired participants. BH-FDR within each family (three conditions; three pairs per test). Sensitivity: path length 2, smoothing 0.02 / 0.5, `--drop-review` (all in the CSVs).

Result: nothing exceeds chance. Within condition: VR Art p = .65, VR Only p = .16, Control p = .41 (FDR .65 / .49 / .62). Between conditions: pre all-three p = .36, post all-three p = .63, all pairs p >= .21 (FDR >= .49). Interaction: all p >= .24 (FDR >= .71). Observed JSDs are around 0.6 bits, the same as under the null: with 16-23 transitions per graph, two graphs built from random subsets of participants differ about as much as any two observed ones.

## Distance between the pre topics and the post topics (`topic_distance.py`, ~4 min; figure `topic_distance.*` shows only S within and between conditions, `topic_distance_detail.*` the topic-pair heat map, chance distribution and the A / B distances)
Because topics are extracted separately per timepoint, the two topic sets are compared directly in the embedding space. A topic is the unit-length mean embedding of its descriptors; D = 1 - cosine between every pre and post topic centre. Per topic: nearest counterpart in the other timepoint and its distance. Set distance S = size-weighted mean nearest-counterpart distance (both directions), M = mean distance under the optimal one-to-one matching. Per condition: A = mean distance from post descriptors to the nearest pre topic, B = the reverse. Chance reference: pre/post labels swapped at random within each participant observed at both timepoints, both topic models refitted (UMAP + HDBSCAN, identical settings; the direct fit reproduces the BERTopic clusters exactly), statistics recomputed (1000 refits). Between conditions: A and B differences with condition labels permuted (topics fixed). BH-FDR within families of three. Outputs: `topic_distance_tests.csv`, `_per_topic.csv`, `_matrix.csv`, `_null.npz`, `_audit.json`.

Additional per-condition set distance S_c: the condition's own descriptors define each topic's centre (topics fixed, topics with fewer than 2 descriptors of the condition left out); its chance reference is the same within-participant swap with refitted topics. Between conditions (labels permuted across participants, topics fixed): differences in S_c, A and B.

Results: the post topics are further from the pre topics than chance (S = .090 vs chance mean .067, p = .020; M = .147 vs .098, p = .017), yet most topics have a close counterpart (Anxious & nervous - Anxiety & fear .04, Calm & tranquil - Feeling calmer .05, Feeling well - Feeling well .04). Topics without a close counterpart: pre "Physical discomfort" (nearest post topic .20, just above the chance 95th percentile .195) and, below the threshold, post "Video & nature" (.18, nearest pre topic Relaxed) and "Emotion & unease" (.18).
* Within condition (S_c, chance mean in brackets): VR Art .158 (.115), p = .002, FDR .006; VR Only .145 (.116), p = .051, FDR .076; Control .110 (.096), p = .124, FDR .124. The S_c values are larger than the overall S because condition-specific topic centres rest on few descriptors and are noisier; each is compared with its own chance reference. A and B (post descriptors to the nearest pre topic, and the reverse) are not significant in any condition (smallest p = .06, VR Only B, FDR .19).
* Between conditions: no difference. S_c: VR Art vs Control +.048 (p = .098, FDR .29), VR Only vs Control +.035 (p = .21, FDR .32), VR Art vs VR Only +.013 (p = .60); A and B: p >= .074 (FDR >= .22).
* Sensitivity (`--drop-review --tag _sens_no_review`, 500 refits, topics refitted on the remaining participants): S is still further than chance (p = .016) but M is not (p = .30); within condition S_c is significant in VR Art (FDR .012) and Control (FDR .036), VR Only p = .056; between conditions all p >= .074 for S_c and A, and one nominal B difference (VR Art vs VR Only p = .040, FDR .12).

## One set of clusters, labels and parameters (`timepoint_topic_labels.py`, `check_topic_consistency.py`)
The clusters of each timepoint are written once by `fit_timepoint_topics.py` and read by every consumer: the core networks and topic distance here, `../topic_network_pre/` and `../topic_network_post/`. The BERTopic parameters and the cluster labels live in `timepoint_topic_labels.py` (imported everywhere) and are the same for pre and post. `python3 check_topic_consistency.py` verifies that the clusters, labels and parameters are identical in these places and that the central topic of every condition and timepoint in `core_network_pre_vs_post.pdf` equals the one in `topic_network_pre` / `topic_network_post` (all checks pass). The former `topic_network_post/` analysis of the full post interview (three questions, 12 topics of its own) is preserved as `../topic_network_post_allquestions/`.
